What to Do Before a Technician Arrives

Put people before equipment. If there is a fire, heavy smoke, a strong gas odor, sparking near water, or another immediate danger, move staff and guests away and call 911 or the appropriate utility emergency line from a safe location. Do not re-enter or attempt a repair yourself.

Smoking Fryer or Hot Equipment

Stop using the unit. If the controls and dedicated gas shutoff or electrical disconnect are clearly identified and safely reachable without crossing smoke, flames, hot oil, or water, turn the equipment off. Never use water on a grease fire, never move a hot fryer, and do not remove access panels.

Gas Odor or Suspected Leak

Do not light burners, create a spark, or operate electrical switches. Leave the affected area, warn others, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Only close a gas valve if emergency personnel or the utility instruct you and it can be reached safely.

Leaking Dishwasher or Ice Machine

Keep people away from the wet floor. If the labeled water shutoff is accessible without stepping into standing water or reaching near energized equipment, close it. Shut off power only at a dry, safely accessible disconnect. Do not reach behind the machine or into an electrical compartment.

Warm Cooler, Freezer, or Walk-In

Keep doors closed, record the displayed and product temperatures, and move perishable food to a working cooler or approved cold storage when it can be done safely. Follow your food-safety plan and local health guidance; do not rely on smell or appearance to decide whether food is safe.

Unusual Noise, Arcing, or Burning Smell

Stop using the equipment and keep the area clear. If a dedicated disconnect is identified, dry, and safely reachable, turn it off. Do not repeatedly reset breakers, bypass a safety control, or restart a unit that is smoking, arcing, or giving off a burning odor.

Preserve Useful Details

After the area is safe, take a photo of the display, error code, model plate, and any visible leak location. Note what happened just before the failure. Do not clear error history, disassemble the unit, or disturb burned wiring and leaking components.

What to Have Ready When You Call

A clear first report helps us judge urgency, prepare the diagnostic visit, and avoid losing time to basic identification after arrival.

  • Equipment identity: the equipment type, brand, model number, and serial number if the rating plate is visible without moving or opening the unit.
  • Exact symptom: what the machine is doing or not doing, when the problem started, and whether it failed suddenly or became worse over time.
  • Operational impact: whether this is a total stoppage, an intermittent failure, or reduced output, and which menu, sanitation, or cold-storage functions are affected.
  • Warnings: any error codes, temperature readings, unusual smells, smoke, leaks, sparks, or new grinding, buzzing, or clicking sounds.
  • Current status: whether the equipment is still running, has been shut down, or cannot be approached safely.
  • Site details: the Memphis-area service address, an on-site contact, access instructions, and the best entrance for the technician.
