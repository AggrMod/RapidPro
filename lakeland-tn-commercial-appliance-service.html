Commercial Appliance Service for Lakeland Kitchens

Commercial Oven and Range Service

An oven that runs hot, cooks unevenly, or will not ignite needs measurements before parts are selected. We compare setpoint with cavity temperature, watch the ignition and flame-proving sequence, inspect burner ports and gas flames, and test thermostats, probes, safety switches, contactors, elements, and convection motors as the equipment design requires.

Commercial Fryer Service

Slow recovery, temperature overshoot, nuisance shutdowns, and failed ignition can come from different systems. We time heat recovery, verify oil temperature independently, and inspect the pilot or electronic ignition, burners or elements, temperature probe, operating control, high-limit circuit, drain valve, and filter return so the diagnosis follows the symptom.

Commercial Dishwasher Service

When racks emerge dirty, cold, or incompletely rinsed, we trace the machine through fill, wash, drain, heat, and final-rinse stages. Checks can include water level, strainers, spray arms, pump pressure, tank heaters, booster temperature, rinse flow, and drain components. On chemical-sanitizing units, we inspect pickup tubing and feed operation separately from mechanical wash faults.

Ice Machine Service and Cleaning

Low output, thin cubes, long freeze times, and harvest failures call for a full cycle observation. We check water entry and distribution, the pump, evaporator ice pattern, mineral scale, condenser airflow, fan operation, harvest controls, and bin control. Cleaning addresses sanitation and scale; refrigeration readings are added when cycle behavior points to the sealed system.

Commercial Refrigeration Service

For a warm reach-in, prep table, or other commercial refrigerator, we record cabinet temperature and observe the operating cycle. We inspect door seals, product clearance, condenser and evaporator airflow, coil condition, fan motors, defrost parts, drains, and temperature controls before using electrical or refrigerant-circuit readings to narrow the fault.

Preventative Appliance Service

Service intervals should follow actual conditions in the kitchen. We consider daily run time, cook-line heat, grease exposure, water scale, condenser location, and service history; document temperatures and component condition; and separate immediate failures from cleaning, wear, or adjustments that can be scheduled before they interrupt service.

How do you diagnose commercial oven and fryer problems?

We verify actual temperature and recovery, observe the ignition or electric heating sequence, and test operating controls and safety circuits as applicable. For fryers, we also check oil level, the drain, and filter-return flow because heating and circulation problems can produce similar cooking complaints.

How do you diagnose dishwashers, ice machines, and refrigeration equipment?

Dishwasher diagnosis follows the fill, wash, drain, heat, rinse, and sanitizing sequence. Ice-machine and refrigeration diagnosis can include water-flow, scale, temperature, airflow, electrical, defrost, and cycle checks, with sealed-system testing used when those findings indicate a refrigerant-circuit problem.

How often should Lakeland commercial appliances be serviced?

The right interval depends on operating hours, grease exposure, water quality, ambient heat, condenser location, and equipment type. High-use cooking equipment, ice machines exposed to scale, and refrigeration near a cook line may benefit from quarterly checks; an initial inspection can establish a practical interval for each unit.
