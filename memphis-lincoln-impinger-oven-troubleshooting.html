Before you call — what can you actually check?

Written by Terry Jamison, Lead Technician · EPA 608 Universal Certified Updated September 11, 2026

You make food for a living, not ovens — that's fine. Most of what goes wrong on a Lincoln Impinger needs a technician, and this guide says so honestly. But one check in particular takes 30 seconds and matters for the safety of the unit. Jump to your issue below.

Find your issue

  1. Oven not heating up at all
  2. Food cooking unevenly
  3. Conveyor belt won't move
📞 Usually a technician issue

Lincoln Impinger oven not heating up at all

This is the single most common issue we get called about on Lincoln ovens. Most of the time it's a failed heating element — a real part failure, not a setting or a reset. That part isn't a DIY swap.

But there's one real safety check worth doing yourself first: make sure the internal fan is actually blowing air inside the oven. If it's not moving air, turn the oven off immediately. A dead fan lets the electrical components overheat internally — not a fire-out-the-front kind of danger, but real damage to the unit the longer it keeps running.

If the fan is blowing fine and it's still just not heating up, that's almost certainly the heating element(s). Call for service and mention it's not heating at all with the fan running fine — that tells the technician what to bring on the truck.

✅ Safe to check yourself

Food cooking unevenly (one side different than the other)

Check the internal fan the same way as above — uneven cooking is the telltale symptom of a fan that's not moving air properly inside the oven. If the fan isn't blowing, turn the oven off immediately and call for service; running it further risks overheating the internal electrical components.

📞 Call a technician

Conveyor belt won't move

Same as on other conveyor ovens — don't force it, and don't keep restarting the unit hoping it clears itself. Call for service and describe exactly what you're seeing (belt won't move at all vs. moving slow vs. stopping partway) so the technician can narrow it down before arriving.

⚠️ When in doubt, don't guess

If you smell gas, turn off the gas supply immediately and evacuate — don't attempt to fix a gas leak yourself. Beyond the fan check above, heating elements, ignition, and internal electronics are technician territory. A wrong guess usually costs more than the service call would have.
