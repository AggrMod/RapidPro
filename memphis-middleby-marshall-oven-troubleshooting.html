Before you call — what can you actually check?

Written by Terry Jamison, Lead Technician · EPA 608 Universal Certified Updated September 11, 2026

You make food for a living, not ovens — that's fine. Most of what goes wrong on a Middleby Marshall needs a technician, and this guide says so honestly. But a few things take 30 seconds to check and might get you back running tonight. Jump to your issue below.

Find your issue

  1. Screen won't light up / no display
  2. "High control compartment temperature" warning
  3. "Front Belt Jam" (or "Rear Belt Jam") alert
  4. Is the flame on my burner normal?
  5. Conveyor won't move, but no jam alert is showing
  6. Oven won't reach temperature
  7. Pizzas coming out under- or over-cooked
✅ Safe to check yourself

Screen won't light up / no display at all

Check that it's actually plugged in first. If it is, check that the front and back panels/doors are fully closed and seated flush — not loose or slightly ajar. These ovens have safety interlock switches behind the panels that all have to be fully pressed in before the unit will power on at all. Middleby Marshall's own manuals call this out as "LOWER SW NOT CLOSED" when it's the culprit — a panel that looks closed but isn't quite seated is the most common cause, not a wiring problem.

✅ Safe to check yourself

"High control compartment temperature" warning

The front fan filters need cleaning, or replacing if they're damaged. Left dirty, the oven keeps overheating its own control compartment and shutting itself down to protect the electronics inside. This one's safe to check and clean yourself — no tools, no risk.

✅ Safe to check yourself

"Front Belt Jam" (or "Rear Belt Jam") alert

Most often you'll see this as "Front Belt Jam." These ovens can flag either the front or rear conveyor section separately, so you may see either alert depending on which one it is — the response is the same either way. Turn the oven off first. Look for anything visibly stuck in the conveyor — a pan, packaging, debris. If you can see it and safely remove it, clear it and restart.

If there's nothing visibly stuck, or the alert comes right back — stop. That's a technician call, not something to keep restarting and hoping. A real jam alert on these units shouldn't fire unless there's an actual obstruction, or the belt's speed controller is sending the motor voltage but not getting confirmation feedback back — which points to a sensor problem, not something you can see or fix by hand.

⚠️ Know the difference

Is the flame on my burner normal?

Normal: small flames coming out of the row of little round holes across the face of the burner. That's exactly how it's designed to burn — nothing wrong there.

Not normal — turn the oven off immediately: if you see an open flame or fire coming through a crack or gap between the two burners, not the round holes, the burner itself has failed. Don't run the oven again until a technician replaces it. That's a real hazard, not a cosmetic issue.

📞 Call a technician

Conveyor won't move, but no "belt jam" alert is showing

This usually points to a broken gear in the belt drive, not a jam — the belt itself will move freely by hand when you check it, which is different from the machine simply refusing to run. Don't force anything. Call for service and mention there's no jam alert showing — that one detail tells the technician it's likely mechanical, not an obstruction, before they even walk in the door.

📞 Mostly a technician issue

Oven won't reach temperature

One thing worth checking first: make sure both the BLOWER and HEAT switches are actually turned on — easy to bump one by accident.

If they're on and it's still not heating, this needs a technician. It could be the flame sensor, the ignition module, or the inducer motor and its damper not opening (and returning to position) correctly — all of these need diagnostic tools to tell apart, not something to guess at or take apart yourself.

✅ Safe to adjust yourself

Pizzas coming out under- or over-cooked (bake time seems off)

The bake time / belt speed setting is right on the oven's control panel — this one you can adjust yourself. The rule: if food is coming out too fast (undercooked), raise the time setting; if it's coming out too slow (overcooked), lower it. Nudge it in about 20% steps and check the next pie — don't guess wildly, walk it toward the target.

Example: if you're set for 6:30 but pizzas are actually coming out in 4 minutes, try raising the setting to 7 or 8 minutes and see if the real result moves closer to correct.

What happens next tells you something useful either way:

  • If adjusting it makes a real difference — that's a legitimate stopgap for now, but it usually means the motor's internal Hall effect sensor is failing. That part can't be replaced on its own; eventually the motor (and most likely the speed controller with it) will need replacing. Tell the technician "adjusting the time setting did change the actual result" — that's a real diagnostic clue for them.
  • If adjusting it does nothing at all — the drive isn't responding to commands at all, which points to a more complete failure. Worth telling the technician exactly that, too.

Either way it's heading toward a service call, but adjusting the time setting yourself in the meantime is legitimate and safe.

⚠️ When in doubt, don't guess

If you smell gas, turn off the gas supply immediately and evacuate — don't attempt to fix a gas leak yourself. For anything involving the burner, ignition, or internal electronics beyond what's listed above, that's technician territory. Middleby Marshall WOW Series ovens are precise machines — a wrong guess usually costs more than the service call would have.
